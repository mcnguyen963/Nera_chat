# Story So Far

## IY 731: The Fen fire, inherited power, and the first household bonds

On 18/09/731, eleven-year-old Nera Veyrath intervened after Liora Fen accidentally set fire to Lady Ilyra Veyrath's locked rooms while recovering a keepsake. Ilyra's final portrait was destroyed, Elise Fen was injured, and a shipping folio disappeared. Nera prevented the prepared punishment but accepted a seven-year sentence of unpaid service for Liora in his wing, ending when she turned eighteen; Elise entered household employment. Nera regarded his intervention as protection, whereas Liora never forgot that her sentence was imposed, not chosen. The missing folio exposed trafficking through forged debts and magistrate seals, including twenty-three free people in a transaction set, a forty-one-name allocation, and a private ledger. Krail helped Nera recognize its significance; Nera concealed it, and Isolde Veyless later kept it. The underlying trafficking remained unresolved.

During the Greyford affair, Nera bought the foxkin Vesper Nox for two gold six and obscured her purchase price through household classification. Vesper remembered the distinction between discretion and freedom. Mistress Aldissa Vane discovered Nera's exceptional rune perception and Rift-script ability, concealed them from Cassian, and helped teach him. Nera developed extraordinary physical strength and speed, controlled aether attacks, and the perfected Heaven Splitter, deliberately suppressing his full capabilities. His later two-year command of Blackwing forces in the Eastern Marches included conquest, destroyed villages, killed noncombatants, and deliberate terror; some abuses were ordered by Nera and others permitted by him. The military treasury remained the Banner's money, not his personal purse.

## House Arvayne, the imperial court, and divided loyalties

Harl & Merrow trapped House Arvayne in debt; Cassian Veyrath acquired its largest claim. Nera earned imperial attention and the designation Attended during a capital visit; the Emperor noticed his link to Ilyra's maternal lineage. Cassian later used Nera to deliver a sealed cordial box to Lord Arvayne while arranging Arvayne's death. Nera knew assassination was intended, did not know the contents or method, warned Arvayne that death approached, and nevertheless completed the delivery. Arvayne died four days later, officially of illness. The mechanism and box's actual role remained unproved. Celestine Arvayne became a Veyrath ward without Nera having arranged it. He promised her one honest answer to an important question upon her eighteenth birthday; it remained owed. Celestine increasingly pursued independent responsibility through accounting, correspondence, and Academy work. Princess Katarina Aurent separately allowed Nera one spoken question when older; it was not an offer of assistance and remained unused. Nera opposed many of Cassian's methods while seeking his own banner, soldiers, and political authority.

Over the concealed years, Liora received useful training from Nera's tutors Dovan Kirk, Hult, and Vane, but instruction did not erase her compulsory service. Elise funded Liora's Academy place with her own wages. During Nine Hollows, Liora's concealed soul-fire manifested to protect Nera, its nature remaining secret. Vesper developed unusual seam-cutting and perception; privately she showed Nera she had nine tails, eight hidden from ordinary magical perception. Only Nera knew. Isolde became Nera's magically bonded protector and meticulous day-book keeper. Although compelled to obey commands, she maintained that her personal devotion, her chosen ring worn on a cord, and what she did when not ordered could be voluntary. Nera's imposed designation of her as his “Weapon” was not her freely chosen identity.

## Crownspire Academy: skill, consent, and professional independence

Nera entered Crownspire in IY 738 after an eleven-day journey, ranking second behind Alaric Dawnmere. Alaric challenged him to prove what kind of noble he would become over four years. In Group 3 with Alaric, Sylwem Aelthir, Rhea Calder, Liora, and Evelyne Corven, he helped build a winter-rated roadside shelter using Sylwem's living-wood geometry. Nera severed two foreign-pattern anchors in one uninterrupted line, exposing abnormal rune knowledge. Sylwem discovered legally imported Elaranth timber had originated in seized forests under fraudulent imperial documents, submitted her own attestation, and refused to let Nera speak for her. The shelter entered the charter finders' list with Liora's drainage correction: the second-spur feature was a silt sump, not a fault. Sylwem subsequently closed a personal possibility Nera had failed twice to answer; her eventual return to Elaranth remained a real constraint.

Various peers learned to respect Nera's ability without submitting to him. Malfoid Valecourt discovered his crystal illusions could not reliably mislead Nera; Wen Halloran showed that recovery and positioning could matter as much as force; Garen Mornstahl valued technique but refused family-based obligations; Vayne Kettwald accepted Nera's refusal of another bout. Riven Morcant declined immediate recruitment under Nera's banner and said to ask after four years of meaningful work. Artoria Pendragon accepted honest technical cooperation while retaining the right to object publicly. Fleurdelys Aelwyn, constrained by a lake-court compact she had not signed herself, worked with Nera on a three-floating-blade technique. He helped correct her returning blades; she refused his attempted recruitment framing, and he apologized. By 27/10 she could recover the third blade correctly three times and requested a harder exercise, with no confirmed completion yet.

In the Asterfen field-command exercise, Nera led the third-draw team under Severin Holt in the registered order Rhea (reconnaissance), Lysandra Aurent (forward fighter), Nera (ranged middle), Ilse Vael-Castellan (wards), Kujou Sara (rear ranged), Malfoid (rear security); there was no healer. He declared a minimum of 200 condensed beams daily without exhaustion, not a maximum, which Holt recorded as unverified; Rhea required advance warning before shots. The team completed ridge wards, recovered three sealed packets, traversed the safer western rib, rescued the simulated fracture casualty by the dry route, evacuated Halden Rusk, suffered no casualties, and placed first. Nera nevertheless tried a formation change without consulting affected teammates and gave Sara conflicting rescue assignments. Both corrections were accepted and recorded. The exercise established his competence alongside a tendency to rearrange others without respecting their roles.

## Relationships and personal boundaries before the excursion

Liora became Nera's girlfriend while insisting her adulthood was independent of his household authority. A signed page affirmed her choice at eighteen and required advance communication about consequential changes involving his other romantic relationships; it created no exclusivity or veto over anyone else. A missed corridor appointment during Vesper's registry matter made follow-through especially important. Nera previously made a reproductive decision without discussing it with Liora; she required prior discussion of decisions affecting her body. No pregnancy was established. On 21/10 a private confrontation over a refused intimate request escalated when Nera invoked his Veyrath upbringing and threatened to leave. Liora acknowledged he had once helped her but rejected the idea that this canceled seven unpaid years. She also recognized that controlling every aspect of their encounters had sometimes become a barrier to closeness. She confessed long-held love and voluntarily allowed a more yielding private dynamic while retaining privacy, contraception, bodily control, and the right to stop. Their reconciliation was real, not a surrender of autonomy.

Vesper rejected postponing an answer about their relationship until graduation and became Nera's girlfriend on negotiated terms. She secured a protected-student clause naming Nera personally, rather than the estate, as her holder of record; reassignment needed his countersignature or a lawful tribunal order with notice. This made him accountable without freeing her. Vesper obtained her owed truth-fee about Lord Arvayne, learning Nera's account of the assassination and keeping it private. Nera told her he loved her; she received that declaration seriously but did not reciprocate the words. She retained control over public affection, intimate boundaries, contraception, privacy, access to her tails, and departures; she rejected possessive behavior in public. Liora and Vesper accepted consensual nonexclusive romance without a first-wife hierarchy.

Isolde struggled with an even deeper imbalance: her bond required obedience whether she agreed or not. She distinguished this from personally choosing to stay beside Nera or love him. She objected when he demanded she choose between an obedient weapon and an independent girl while the bond itself precluded a clean choice. On 22/10 she insisted her planned Vharos registry appearance be alone, lest a clerk record her as brought by a Veyrath holder. Nera could accompany her only to the outer step. Vesper separately insisted her excursion day be chosen after seeing the city, not planned for her in advance.

Lysandra combined competitive respect with guarded personal interest. At the Asterfen debrief she accepted operational facts without granting personal trust. In the west practice court on 21/10, she and Nera sparred with blunt steel: an initial draw, a later disarm by Nera, then a pass in which his sword-flat struck her shoulder. She declared, “Pass to you. Ground to you. Not yielded.” Two witnesses might report facts; she would not lie about the result or blade burr. At dinner she learned of Kirk, Hult, and Vane, but considered Nera's explanation of his unusual limits incomplete, observing restraint was a decision. She applied for her own third-bell cold-floor tower hour and obtained it on Monday 24/10. She also accepted the possibility of another dinner only if Nera cooked. Her conditional promise—“When the ground has held twice, I will give you the other answer. Not before.”—remained unanswered even after acknowledging the ground had held twice.

Lysia Solenne trained Nera in precise infirmary reporting and challenged him to distinguish charity from consent. She came from a Soleris wash-house family; the Church claimed her healing talent and put her in the novitiate at eleven before she could choose, but she made healing her own duty. She kept the Cinder Saintess title, the Soleris burning, and related records out of informal conversation, directing formal inquiries to the east wing. Nera worked an infirmary audit shift on 22/10, correcting stock discrepancies rather than hiding them. At dinner he confessed his Eastern Marches atrocities, including knowing orders against civilians. Lysia refused absolution and asked whether he truly renounced such conduct. He promised only not to resort to it lightly, from convenience, anger, or pride, and to bear its consequences, not that he could never face such choices. She valued the less evasive answer without treating it as reassurance. She would heal wounded people regardless of allegiance and not promise compliance with orders harming civilians. Liora knew Nera was interested in Lysia.

The optional Crownspire Vharos excursion departed Friday 28/10, arrived Saturday 29/10 at 18:20, and scheduled return Tuesday 01/11; it had thirty-six seats, school escort restrictions, and a fee of two crowns six each. Nera filed for himself and Vesper; Liora paid and filed for herself, and Isolde filed in her own hand using her wages. Vesper's Class B listing said “Bonded under the clause.” Nera initially mistook the five-day travel itinerary for four city days, promising Liora time that did not exist. After correction, Sunday 30/10 afternoon/evening belonged to Liora following the mandatory arena tour; Monday 31/10 10:00–14:00 was reserved for Isolde's independent registry visit; Vesper chose the remaining Monday afternoon and evening, without pretending that a partial day was the promised full one.

The authorized district covered much of the west basin, not the remote refuse slope. Nera sought less visible escorting and signed a personal-movement indemnity for all four, agreeing to declared districts and hours, responsibility for their movements, a rooftop observer, and a stricter 22:00 curfew. The under-officer did not waive safety rules wholesale. Liora, Vesper, and Isolde retained separate decisions about public affection, purchases, and movement.

On Sunday, Nera and Liora spent their chosen time in ordinary markets rather than noble spectacle. Liora paid her own way where she chose, bought gifts and an imperfect red-glass bead, accepted some freely chosen affection, and split the cost of a private room at the Ochre Lamp. Their private time followed established consent and contraception agreements. They returned before curfew. Liora considered this specific day fulfilled and rejected any attempt to turn it into an outstanding romantic debt. She retired unaware of Nera's subsequent plan.

## The clandestine rescue of Dara and Starlia — 30–31/10/738

On Sunday night Nera told Isolde he needed to help two people in Vharos, ordered her to assist, and took her outside the permitted district toward the lower holding-yard refuse slope. She warned of the excursion indemnity, kidnapping risks, paperwork exposure, and possible danger to the other students, but the magical bond compelled compliance. Isolde arranged a room above an oil press, physician, supplies, and runners. Beneath a canvas beside a sign reading “SEX FOR FOOD,” they found Dara Joe, an emaciated former elf heavy knight with eleven years' service and two years in captivity, guarding Starlia Marin, whom she had protected for about six months. Starlia was critically malnourished, infected, feverish, mutilated, nearly voiceless, and close to death.

Nera offered both women treatment, shelter, food, safety, employment for Dara, and revenge. In return he demanded Starlia's remaining life as his personal possession and authority over her future. Dara denounced the resemblance to slavers' offers and insisted on medical help before negotiating. Starlia expressed fragmentary agreement while severely ill and unable to leave or bargain freely. Nera treated that acceptance as a binding transaction. The physician judged her left forearm and both legs gangrenous, her right hand badly damaged, and ordinary healing insufficient to replace nutrition or blood. Nera authorized emergency operations. Around 01:40 Monday 31/10, Starlia survived amputations of both legs higher than hoped and the left forearm near the elbow, with further right-hand tissue loss; only three fingers remained usable. Her fever, infection, and postoperative weakness remained life-threatening. Nera paid the initial physician charges (sixty silver, then forty for return assessment), left money for treatment and supplies, and accepted correction when his proposed food was unsafe to swallow. Isolde and Nera secretly returned to the hostel around 02:20; the absence initially went unnoticed. Immediate transfer to Crownspire was abandoned in favor of care until a physician cleared travel and lawful, supervised transportation could be arranged.

On Monday, Isolde entered Vharos's bonded registry alone. The clerk initially treated her as a holder's representative, but she insisted on a dated filing in her own name. It documented the conflict between her clause-bound legal status under House Veyrath and her personal entitlement to Crownspire wages; it did not free her from either the legal record or magical bond. She left at 13:35, having established documentary evidence of her independent appearance rather than obtained a new freedom certificate.

Vesper chose her Monday date: ordinary caravan-yard food and watching the lamps from the basin wall. A memory of Ravensong at age eight surfaced, the last city lights she remembered watching without being required elsewhere. When Nera revealed he had nearly exhausted his personal purse overnight, Vesper covered food and demanded a handwritten accounting later. He then disclosed Starlia and his plan to treat her as a cherished possession, even a decoration, eliminating many decisions she otherwise might make. Vesper condemned the ownership framing, recalling that Nera had purchased her at Greyford and that “protection” had not made her free. She asked to speak with Starlia alone; Nera allowed it. The excursion office confirmed the oil-press room lay within the authorized district, one street before the second bell-post, though the refuse slope did not. Vesper criticized Nera's certainty about geography he had not checked.

In private Vesper asked Starlia for her name and what she wanted. Starlia named herself and identified a purchaser whose actions haunted her. Nera then heard Starlia describe a man with a red-stone ring who had paid one gold for her, while a woman in a grey coach observed and nodded. Dara had not known of the woman. Nera recorded the ring, price, and coach, promised the man's death within three days, and said he knew but would not reveal the woman’s identity, intending to act later. Dara stressed that an actual name, link to a house or seal, and evidence mattered more than an unidentified killing.

## Starlia's contract and the cost of clear words

Starlia resisted being called an object in Nera's room. She said a debt could be worked off but a thing could not, and demanded the bargain's duration, obligations, payments, purchase price, and signatories be written rather than left to Nera's future interpretation. Nera refused a finite service term; he claimed her entire remaining life, control over residence and duties, and a prohibition against suicide or intentional self-injury. Dara offered her own military service in exchange for Starlia's release; Starlia rejected substitution. She instead demanded the complete humiliating truth be written as a confession of the buyer's obligations as well as hers, publicly read before a court while she was alive and before the end of IY 738. Nera accepted a signed written account and public reading, warning the system might find such cruelty lawful. Starlia's eventual signature and Dara's witness signature were deferred until Starlia recovered enough to hear and execute the document; Dara required an independent copy.

By Tuesday morning Nera told Starlia plainly there was no guaranteed route from her bargain to life somewhere other than his room. Starlia observed that an injured woman without money, limbs, or viable alternative was not choosing as Liora had been able to choose upon turning eighteen. She demanded her name, “Starlia Marin,” appear before any price. Nera gave her Corvan Hess's ring as proof the first identified perpetrator was dead, said he would protect her against future threats, and offered Dara a paid household guard post. He proposed eventual prosthetics, rehabilitation, and possibly Academy study, but Dara exposed practical gaps: Starlia could not travel, Dara's own arm was splinted, and no helper in Crownspire could assist a bedridden woman in Vharos. Starlia also challenged his talk of service within a week when she could barely move, and of scholarship access without known talent or someone to write for her.

When asked what service he expected, Nera answered “Sexual service.” Dara demanded that he clarify the term; Starlia, calculating from desperation, agreed while insisting the precise wording be written in her own voice for public hearing. Her statement included: “I am yours. The whole of it, all of it, until one of us is finished. Use me however you want and don't ask me again, because being asked is worse than being decided.” This was a spoken acceptance under extreme dependence, not proof of equal bargaining or unrestricted consent. Dara demanded an hour alone with Starlia without Nera deciding her meaning. Nera withdrew. He hired Perla, a local widow, at two silver weekly for washing and lifting morning and evening, and Marta Sedge, the cutter's assistant, as a nurse at two silver daily. Dara later orally accepted guarding Starlia at eighty silver monthly, potentially rising to two gold, but required guard duties, additional cleaning/care, pay, and helpers written plainly. She refused a promise to perform unsafe solo lifting.

Nera amended and signed the contract with Starlia's name first, her explicit statement, his commitments, price and lifelong claim, and Dara's employment. He gave it to Dara for Starlia to hear after food and sleep, amend if she wished, and sign with witnesses only when ready. Dara objected to treating fever-bed statements like sworn testimony, insisted on independently copying the document, and demanded her own duties not be obscured as “duties as set out.” Liora read it twice: “It's honest. That's not the same as safe.” Nera authorized corrections and left both copies in Dara's keeping pending later review. **Starlia had not signed, Dara had not witnessed or finished the independent copy, and the court hearing had not been scheduled.** The bargain's validity and consequences remained unresolved.

## Hess's killing, testimony, and the return north

Late Monday 31/10 Nera ordered Isolde to identify and kill the red-stone purchaser. Isolde warned about time, legal exposure, and noble connections but obeyed. At 21:52 she returned with the ring, reporting the man was Corvan Hess, an ordinary third-pen factor buying people on others' accounts; the ring bore a merchant mark, not noble arms. She killed him near the fourth bell-post and made the scene resemble a robbery. The third pen's docket book was still in its drawer when she checked it; she did not remove it. Nera kept the ring concealed on his person. Isolde's clandestine return left a hostel entry discrepancy later partly addressed by writing herself into the morning list, without clearing the night omission. She recorded Hess's death tersely in her day-book and warned she would not lie to Starlia if directly questioned. Nera ordered truthful minimal answers to watch inquiries, without volunteering Hess or day-book details.

On Tuesday 01/11, Starlia survived another night with fever high but stable, and no visible blackened stitch line. Vesper's desired return visit conflicted with the 08:00 coach departure. Nera could not take a private coach independently; instead the excursion master authorized a 09:00 rear baggage party at his expense, with three students registered to visit the oil-press room from 06:15 to 08:45 and then rejoin the column. Vesper spoke with Starlia first, independently; Liora insisted on knowing the story and saw the situation herself rather than relying on Nera's edited account. Nera finally disclosed the contract, medical state, the dead purchaser, and Isolde's role to Liora. She warned his status would not protect him against hostile uses of his signed document and demanded he tell ugly facts early. Starlia asked Liora whether Liora stayed with Nera voluntarily after her sentence; Liora answered, “Both. And the wanting came later than the staying,” explaining that what mattered was having a genuine option to leave.

The group caught the 09:00 rear party and rejoined the main column at the chain-walk wayhouse. Vesper delivered a considered verdict: Nera had told Starlia uncomfortable truths, agreed to her name first, hired competent help, and left when Dara demanded privacy. Yet he had offered sexual ownership to a profoundly helpless woman at the worst hour of her life. Vesper opposed the act without rejecting his concrete provision of care: “Not a monster. Something worse to argue with—a man who's very good at making a thing sound like a bargain, and who means it while he's saying it.” She insisted on witnessing any signature, reading Starlia's words rather than Nera's. Nera accepted her presence and confirmed Starlia would remain in Vharos until fit to travel; Dara, Perla, Marta Sedge, and the cutter would care for her. The medical room, physician, runner, food, and medicines had been formally extended by one week, renewable on the physician's word. Vesper waived interest on her spending related to the rescue but still expected his handwritten account.

At Pell's Cross that evening Nera had no coin and considered another paid room. Liora gave him three silver, then she and Vesper chose to share a room. The Vharos watch had publicly reported Hess robbed and murdered; the third pen was sealed. Isolde had kept the death in her day-book but not invented an alibi. Friday 04/11 at 19:00 became the appointed private financial reckoning among Nera, Liora, Elise, and Isolde. Nera's wing had nominally received **3,000 gold monthly** since he was fourteen into a Vaelcross account, drawn with seals and countersignatures, while he carried a limited personal purse from earlier campaigns. Household expenses were estimated at roughly seventy-odd gold a month, but Cassian's steward could charge estate costs against the junior heir's allowance; **the actual balance was unknown**. The Blackwing treasury was restricted to military needs. Ilyra's separate Vaelcross trust remained sealed, its trustees, value, and release terms unknown. Nera proposed prosthetics from top craftsmen with a provisional **2,000-gold budget**, but accepted he must obtain Krail's figures and an assessment before committing. Liora demanded real numbers, not impressive estimates, and highlighted Elise's occasions buying household soap from her own wages.

## Katarina's identity, the covert plan, and trust strained

In a private meeting of four at Pell's Cross, Nera told Liora, Vesper, and Isolde that **he identified the grey-coach witness as elder Princess Katarina Aurent**, whom he believed involved in a secret, depraved society of young nobles. He would not explain how he knew. He intended to appear as a debauched aristocrat, attract her notice, earn an invitation, and investigate the circle. Part of the motive behind Starlia's lifelong contract, residence in Nera's room, and planned public court reading was to create a reputation suitable for that disguise; he also intended his protected room to make reaching Starlia harder for Katarina. He ordered the three not to tell Starlia or Dara, pursue independent inquiries, or write Katarina's name in the day-book. He insisted Starlia's recovery came first and that he genuinely found her beautiful and might come to love her, yet joked about making his girlfriends jealous.

Vesper was ashamed, not jealous; she had taken Starlia's desperate statements seriously and rejected their use as theatre. Isolde said the girl had not been told she was wearing his costume and emphasized obedience to an order was not agreement. Liora believed his account of a possible political threat but warned, “A costume you can't take off isn't a costume.” She demanded he explain **before Friday's figures** what becomes of Starlia's paper if Katarina never takes the bait. Nera apologized and said secrecy was necessary, that commander and playful student were both genuine, and that judgmental looks from those he trusted hurt. Vesper said disagreement was evidence of caring, not withdrawal; Liora said accuracy mattered more than gentleness. Nera later complained that soldiers trusted his command without questioning him; Vesper reminded him soldiers' obedience under threat was not the same as voluntary trust. The women stayed, but the differences remained.

On Wednesday 02/11 Nera agreed to slow down court plans because Starlia was medically unfit to travel or sign. He had suggested Monday 07/11, but Liora explained no hearing date existed, a magistrate's calendar and notices to named parties were necessary, and filing would create an accessible public record. Nera corrected a mistaken use of “Kara” for Dara. He would tell Starlia about Katarina only when he judged it safe to act, despite Liora and Vesper's insistence that she needed the unvarnished plan before signing. The Vharos watch then reported **the third pen's docket book missing**. Isolde stated she had not taken it, and Nera believed her. He privately suspected Katarina but had no proof; Liora noted another unknown actor was possible and the local watch was unlikely to obtain the writ necessary to investigate effectively. Nera said he did not expect conventional evidence, even extensive testimony, to convict royalty, and did **not** intend a conventional court prosecution against Katarina. Isolde later asked whether he had a real next step beyond getting close and hoping; no further operational plan was disclosed.

At the ford wayhouse Nera signed letters requesting Krail's wing balance and charges since summer; asking the **Ilyra Veyrath Trust** in Vaelcross for “terms of release, heir of record,” not payment; and confirming Vharos care in Starlia Marin's name. Isolde cautioned that addressing an institution without a keeper's personal name depended on an honest clerk, and that a month of silence would itself be informative. The letters were put into the relevant postal/relay channels, with delivery not guaranteed. Isolde recorded significant facts without writing Katarina's name, including the missing docket book. She preserved a difference between posted and actually received correspondence.

## Thursday 03/11/738: Crownspire return, healing, and a new spar

The excursion group reached Crownspire's gate at about 16:10 and the official roll closed. The under-officer warned that promises, purchases, and people acquired south of Sablewater did not enter Academy jurisdiction without authorized field-office papers; Nera had none and said he would not bring anyone in without permission. Isolde arranged to put the closed day-book in the private wing safe rather than a public office tray. Liora reiterated the Friday 04/11 19:00 meeting: shut door, **Starlia's paper and the Katarina contingency first, then accounts**. Krail's reply might have reached the wing or be routed through the field office.

Nera went first to Lysia in the infirmary rather than immediately visiting Lysandra. Lysia inspected his stiff shoulder, saw signs he had witnessed suffering, and asked whether anyone injured was being left untreated. He reported two abused, starving women in Vharos, one losing both legs and her left arm with three fingers left on her right hand; he kept Starlia's name out of the infirmary record. He confirmed nurse, medicine, food, and shelter, admitted he did not know the exact fever measurements or the surgeon's full report, and promised to have those details sent. Lysia warned that future prosthetics required a skilled socket fitter and bone specialist, not merely a smith, because abrasion and infection could kill a survivor months afterward. She wanted the cutter's raw notes **including crossed-out concerns**, the operations, clinical warnings, and fever trend—not invented numbers. She agreed to a hug while establishing three conditions: medical updates must go to her directly, deterioration must be communicated when reported rather than after ordinary delayed delivery, and if she treated Starlia her medical judgment would determine fitness to travel irrespective of Nera's household status. Nera agreed. They hugged, showing personal warmth without Lysia surrendering professional independence. She said she would attend any public reading of Starlia's agreement, for Starlia rather than for Nera.

Nera then visited Lysandra at the west practice court. She reiterated that her formally granted third-bell tower hour was for serious work, not a social visit; their next tower meeting was **three days after the Thursday return**, at her standing hour. Nera proposed both a conversation and an aether-only ranged pass. Lysandra discarded her training sword, prohibited beams above the second ring because the east ward was cracked, and agreed to first blood or first clean hit, with any breach of the ward chalk canceling the pass. She demanded credible strikes rather than conspicuous restraint. While speaking, Nera briefly disclosed rescuing two abused women, a former warrior and a country girl, one severely injured, without identifying them or detailing the covert political plan. Lysandra opened with three ice spears. After initial exchanges Nera fired three aether bolts followed by five staggered shots; Lysandra blocked or dodged seven but took the fifth of the latter sequence on her leading calf. She acknowledged a clean pass to Nera but did **not** yield. She recognized that his sudden silence meant he had deliberately chosen to end the pass; she saw all eight bolts but could not stop them all. She asked whether he had dealt with the perpetrators rather than merely learned to be stronger. Nera answered that one was dead, the others were not all identified, the women were safe and their recovery came first, and killing the guilty did not heal victims. He said he had also come because he missed her, not only to win. Her next tower hour, competitive respect, and promised other answer remained open.

# Characters

## Nera Veyrath

**At the cutoff:** Eighteen-year-old Veyrath heir, Crownspire first-year, former Eastern Marches commander, and concealed catastrophic-level combatant. He had returned from Vharos with no personal coin, a signed but unexecuted lifelong agreement claiming Starlia's service, responsibility for her care, a concealed ring proving Hess's death, and a plan to infiltrate Katarina's secret society. Liora, Vesper, and Isolde knew that plan; Starlia and Dara did not. He faced Friday's accounting, a request for accurate medical records from Lysia, and another tower session with Lysandra.

**Voice:** Confident, formal, direct; affectionate and playful when relaxed, colder when his authority is questioned. Exact source examples: “No. A deal is a deal, and I've already done my part.” “I have nothing to add.” “Both are real. Neither is an act.” “I don't need you to tell me every time you think I'm wrong.”

**Arc:** He repeatedly saved people and provided concrete resources while asserting power over the conditions of their rescue. Asterfen showed he could accept tactical correction; his confession to Lysia showed accountability without absolution. His Starlia bargain repeated coercive structures he claimed to reject. He was capable of genuine attachment and vulnerability but often used secrecy, wealth, and military reasoning to deflect questions of voluntary choice. His central conflict remained whether protection could coexist with meaningful freedom under his authority.

## Liora Fen

**At the cutoff:** Nera's independent girlfriend, childhood companion, and rigorous critic of incomplete explanations. Her Vharos Sunday was fulfilled, but trust was strained by his concealment of Starlia's plight and Katarina's plan. She had examined Starlia's contract, helped Nera when his purse was empty, and organized Friday's closed-door reckoning.

**Voice:** Blunt, precise, grounded in accounts, dates, and bodily autonomy. “That's the part that matters. Not whether you stay. Whether you could go.” “It's honest. That's not the same as safe.” “A costume you can't take off isn't a costume.” “The hill was the easy part.”

**Arc:** She retained gratitude for Nera's childhood intervention without denying her seven-year sentence. She moved toward freely chosen love and greater intimacy while defending the right to leave, consent, contraception, and communication. She opposed transforming affection, money, or formal freedom into a new unspoken obligation. She wanted Nera to show his plans and their consequences before they became irreversible.
