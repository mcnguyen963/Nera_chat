# Asterfall — Future Canon Event Anchors

## Purpose

This document defines the major future events that should remain part of the Asterfall story even when Nera makes very different choices.

These are **canon anchors, not predetermined outcomes**.

The model should steer the story toward these important situations without controlling Nera's decisions or forcing an original route.

The user controls **Nera's decisions, dialogue, intentions, and voluntary actions**. The world controls schedules, institutions, other characters, circumstances, and consequences.

---

# Core Rule

## Hard canon means the situation arrives.  
## Soft canon means the outcome remains open.

A hard-canon event may establish that:

- an examination is held;
- a festival takes place;
- a delegation arrives;
- a treaty reaches renewal;
- House Veyrath must address succession;
- an academy year begins or ends;
- a major political issue must eventually be confronted.

It does **not** establish:

- who wins or loses;
- who Nera supports;
- who Nera loves;
- whether Nera accepts an offer;
- whether an investigation succeeds;
- whether someone dies;
- whether rebellion or war occurs;
- whether Nera follows an original-game route.

The model may steer circumstances toward an anchor, but must never choose Nera's response to it.

---

# Continuity Rules

1. These anchors should occur unless the user explicitly changes the plan or earlier events make their exact form impossible.

2. If an anchor becomes impossible in its original form, preserve its **purpose**, not obsolete details.

3. Solving a problem early must matter. Do not rediscover evidence already known simply because the outline expected it later.

4. Destroyed organizations, dead characters, completed reforms, spent resources, and resolved cases do not regenerate to restore the planned plot.

5. War, murder, sabotage, arrest, uprising, betrayal, and major injury are **not hard canon**. They require live motives, means, opportunity, and unresolved causes.

6. Do not skip months or years without user approval.

7. Crownspire remains the central setting through the first two academy years, though outside politics may reach students through family pressure, documents, visitors, sponsorships, and consequences.

### The Darkening of the Empire

**Threat progression:** missing people and forged contracts → organized trafficking and murdered witnesses → border raids and attacks on students → frontier warfare and coerced mobilization → siege, accountability, and reconstruction. This is a dark fantasy empire, not a harmless school drama, but it is still an academy story with ordinary days, celebrations, friendships, and school politics.

**Hard situation, open resolution:** Serious disappearances, deadly village atrocities, lethal attacks on academy-affiliated people, and border fighting shape the unfolding story. Their perpetrators act independently of Nera. An early, credible intervention can prevent or mitigate a future incident; do not undo successful prevention to force a tragedy. When danger arrives without Nera being present, its aftermath may already be real. Named established characters are never assigned a predetermined death, abduction, victory, betrayal, or forced relationship.

**The wider war:** Limited armed conflict threatens the eastern marches. A full-scale Empire–Varkesh war, the participation of other countries, and the final settlement depend on choices, military interests, and diplomacy. A negotiated peace may stop escalation; it cannot simply erase atrocities already committed.

**Military continuity:** The Empire uses Crown Legions, Ironwall Orders, cavalry and beast cohorts, battle-mages, warders, aether bombard batteries, and expendable **Chain Levies** drawn from enslaved or coerced people. The Veyrath warhost is a separate, smaller, exceptionally trained and heavily equipped force built around Thornwall Shield Legions, Ironthorn Breakers, Blacksteel Lancers, March Beast Riders, fortress logistics, and its rare **Thorn Paladins** and **Dreadlance Cataphracts**. Use these established formations by their correct names. Cassian commands the warhost under the existing house order. Nera's bloodline, personal physical power, Heaven Splitter, and Academy reputation do **not** automatically give him control of troops.

**Investigation and politics:** Multiple interests profit from people being trafficked: forged service-contract brokers, transport merchants, bribed magistrates, forced-labor buyers, mercenaries, and wartime suppliers. Do not make every incident the work of one invincible mastermind. Link conspiracies only when actual evidence supports a link. Neither Varkesh nor Elaranth nor all Imperial officials are uniformly guilty.

### Year-One Excursions and Academic Recess

During the first academy year, Crownspire schedules three distinct excursions: **Vharos, City of Chains and its Grand Arena** (late autumn); **Miravel, City of the Nine Mage Towers** (late winter/early spring); and the **Crownward Leviathan's voyage to the Tideglass Islands** (spring). The itinerary also includes Asterfen, Founders' Festival, the Winter Banner League, Exchange Fortnight, and Crown Trials. Use several short journeys over the full academic year; do not place all three trips in one month. Their departure dates may shift modestly if live events demand it.

The **announcement and availability** of a faculty-sponsored trip are story anchors. **Nera attending, companions joining him, personal investigations, and excursion outcomes are not.** Other students continue to travel, study, compete, and form friendships in his absence. Maintain real costs, escorts, visas or permits where needed, travel time, safety, student club activities, and professional local residents. Not every outing must uncover a trafficking conspiracy; beauty, wonder, pleasure, and quiet school drama are also intentional content.

At the **end of each academic year**, offer a playable recess or graduation departure where students decide whether to return home, go on vacation, work, study, travel elsewhere, or stay near Crownspire. Do not select Nera's destination, assemble a party for him, or skip the holiday without player approval.

## Y1-DF01 — Names Missing from the Workshop Rolls

**Placement:** Following Y1-02; early autumn of Year One.

An apprentice attached to an equipment supplier fails to collect wages. A scholarship student's porter disappears, and a routine workshop invoice contains an unfamiliar labor category. Other students initially treat it as a clerical matter. The Student News Bureau, workshop staff, Student Council, and Disciplinary Committee may each notice different pieces; no organization automatically learns everything.

A quiet network is moving debt-bound workers and vulnerable migrants under forged service warrants. The first clues are mundane: duplicate seals, false destinations, withheld pay, a transport firm's spotless records, and families afraid to report missing relatives.

**Narrator seeds:** a blank name on a receipt, a parent seeking news, rival clubs disagreeing over publication, a sponsor insisting the missing people are legally transferred.

**Open outcomes:** The students may learn nothing, rescue an individual, expose a minor broker, or uncover a broader lead. Do not force Nera to investigate or make the entire network collapsible by one duel.

## Y1-DF02 — The Ash of Kestrel Hollow

**Placement:** Weeks after the Asterfen excursion; a separate incident, **not** an obligatory catastrophe during Y1-03.

Kestrel Hollow, a small eastern road settlement beyond the Academy's immediate jurisdiction, is attacked at night. Houses burn, civilian families are murdered, and other residents are carried away. The survivors give incompatible accounts of uniforms, flags, and the direction of the departing wagons. The Crown first calls it a bandit incident; border officers suspect a raiding company; local families accuse the authorities of concealing the truth.

This is a real atrocity with deaths, displacement, funerals, missing persons, and long-term witnesses—not just a rumor designed to reward a hero. If prior live actions placed effective protection there, adjust the casualty toll and aftermath honestly; never teleport attackers past credible defenses.

**Narrator seeds:** a refugee child searching for a parent, a healer's request, sketches in a News Bureau report, a village registry that records fewer people than the relief camp contains.

**Open outcomes:** The culprit, military involvement, location of captives, and political response require investigation. Do not assign guilt to Varkesh, the Empire, Cassian, or a specific student without evidence.

## Y1-EX01 — Vharos, City of Chains and the Grand Arena

**Timing:** Late autumn of Year One, after Founders' Festival and before the Winter Banner League. **Duration:** Three or four days; a Crownspire-sponsored urban-history, military-culture, and combat-observation excursion. The **school organizes and offers** the visit; Nera's participation is optional. Students who remain at Crownspire have supervised alternative coursework.

**City and atmosphere:** Vharos is an old fortified Imperial city famous for its monumental **Arena of Sable Crowns**, black-stone galleries, triumphal arches, noble viewing terraces, fighter barracks, gambling houses, and busy workshops serving the games. It markets violence as patriotism and entertainment. Outside the arena stand public markets and pleasant inns; behind them are debt courts, chained transport carts, scarred laborers, and a morgue that must account for the dead.

**The cruelty is institutional, not cartoonish:** Famous professional gladiators sometimes enter voluntarily for status and prize money. Others fight under criminal sentence, debt bondage, enslavement, or coercive contracts. Dangerous monster combats, condemned prisoners, lethal tournament rounds, and a ruling elite that applauds suffering expose an Imperial society in which violence is legally sanctioned but not uniformly admired. Some citizens profit; some campaign for changes; many fear challenging the system. Students must not be compelled to enter an unwarded death match as a school exercise.

**Excursion program:**

- Watch a legal championship or demonstration from supervised galleries. A brutal spectacle may be witnessed; students can walk away, protest, or discuss its justification.
- Visit a licensed armsmith hall and review armor, monster-fighting formations, and fighting styles relevant to Crownspire's martial curriculum.
- Meet arena physicians, administrators, professional champions, and the families of workers; accounts of fame, injury, coercion, and medical costs differ.
- Attend a faculty-led ethics hearing about convicted fighters, 'voluntary' debt contracts, and whether a victory bought with captive lives deserves honor.
- Explore optional social scenes: a citywide festival, an informal student wager with harmless stakes, an exhibition by a celebrated sword master, or a tense banquet hosted by an arena patron.

**Dark fantasy seed:** A missing apprentice from the workshop rolls may appear on an arena labor roster or a forged creditor's list. This is a possible clue, **not** proof all Vharos officials share the trafficking conspiracy. Another thread can concern an unrelated ambitious gladiator, a corrupt noble sponsor, a guild dispute, or a proud local reformer.

**Narrator preparation:** Announce the expedition, applications, costs, faculty escorts, and travel plan in advance. Introduce at least one sympathetic person from Vharos who has a life beyond victimhood or villainy. Let the city function even if Nera refuses the trip.

**Open outcomes:** Nera can watch, compete only in any *separately arranged, voluntary and regulated* exhibition, speak to a fighter, investigate abuses, ignore the controversy, make friends, or choose not to travel. The arena city must feel memorable without requiring a rescue or a riot.

## Y1-DF03 — The Vanished Academy Carriage

**Placement:** After Founders' Festival, when visiting schools and sponsors are traveling home.

A supervised carriage from a smaller provincial academy fails to arrive. Several students and attendants are missing; some guards are found murdered beside an abandoned road. A forged transit order allowed the vehicle through an official checkpoint. The kidnapped students are being valued as hostages, skilled magical labor, or leverage against their families—not depicted sexually.

Crownspire faces pressure from parents and competing authorities. The Disciplinary Committee can protect the campus and preserve evidence; it is not a secret police force. Families with titles obtain louder hearings than poor families. A classmate may know a missing student, so the loss has recognizable human weight.

**Narrator seeds:** unfinished letters, a passenger list with one false name, a surviving horse, competing claims of jurisdiction, an academy memorial.

**Open outcomes:** Some captives might be recovered, some might escape themselves, and some might die if no effective action reaches them in time. Nera's participation is never mandatory.

## Y1-EX02 — Miravel, City of the Nine Mage Towers

**Timing:** Between Winter Petitions and the Spring Exchange Fortnight, during a scheduled winter-to-spring academic excursion. **Duration:** Four to six days. Nera may attend or remain at Crownspire; the program is established independently of his decision.

**City and atmosphere:** Miravel was built around nine competing mage towers, each governed by a research order or guild. Warded skybridges link upper terraces, aether-fed lamps illuminate covered avenues, enchanted lifts climb sheer spires, and anti-teleportation or anti-flight wards mark restricted sectors. Public lectures, spell markets, artifice demonstrations, libraries, tea houses, and student competitions make the city beautiful, scholarly, and proudly eccentric. Beneath the brilliance, guild rank, costly licenses, and magical labor contracts exclude many ordinary residents.

**Excursion program:**

- **The Nine Towers Tour:** Students enter approved halls of elemental magic, ward construction, rune scholarship, healing, alchemy, artificing, combat theory, historical records, and aether engineering. Restricted vaults remain restricted.
- **Mage-Tower Practical:** Mixed teams have a supervised puzzle or containment demonstration involving magical measurements, aether stability, and rune logic. Other students can shine through preparation and teamwork; the result is not predetermined by Nera's power.
- **Tower Exhibition Festival:** Rival apprentices display inventions, compete for licenses, or challenge visiting students in warded friendly matches. Tower personalities, petty feuds, salons, and clubs can recur later.
- **The Scholars' Market:** Rare writing instruments, annotated grimoires, lawful reagents, enchanted keepsakes, and inexpensive curiosities offer quieter character moments and real financial choices.
- **Optional evening visit:** Observatory balconies, a magical light procession, rooftop restaurants, and arguments between scholars create warmth and wonder alongside the darker imperial reality.

**Dark fantasy seed:** Rumors concern illegal aether-binding patents, apprentices trapped by punitive service terms, or a tower's research being sold to military contractors. Evidence can connect to existing trafficking or wartime procurement threads *only when supported*; some scandals must be independent. Scholars can oppose exploitation without the entire city being corrupt.

**Continuity:** Nera's ancient script aptitude, Heaven Splitter, and private capabilities are not revealed by the visit unless his own actions make that happen. A scholar might ask a perceptive question, but nobody obtains secret knowledge by narrator fiat. Admission to a tower, a mentor's invitation, or access to rare lore is an opportunity, not a guaranteed reward.

**Open outcomes:** Research contacts, voluntary training, academic rivalries, club interests, magical discoveries, an uneventful enjoyable visit, or a personal refusal are all valid. The city should remain available for later school breaks or independent journeys.

## Y1-EX03 — Voyage of the Crownward Leviathan: The Tideglass Islands

**Timing:** In the spring after Exchange Fortnight, before the final Crown Trials and their preparations become dominant. **Duration:** Approximately one to two weeks including travel by land to an Imperial harbor and several days at sea. The Academy organizes a major maritime and island-culture expedition. Students who decline or cannot travel receive alternate study and social opportunities.

**The great ship:** Crownspire charters the **Crownward Leviathan**, an immense multi-deck, ocean-going Imperial passenger-and-expedition vessel large enough for several hundred students, staff, and sailors. Her long promenade decks, towering masts, rune-braced hull, storm wards, chart room, kitchens, medical ward, secure cargo hold, cabins, lifeboats, and veteran crew make her a **real large ship**, not a small boat. Conventional sail and skilled seamanship do most of the work; aether reinforcement and navigational wards aid but cannot nullify storms, reefs, mechanical faults, or human errors. A captain commands the ship; professors command academic programming; neither grants Nera command automatically.

**Life aboard is an arc:** Departure ceremonies, competing cabin assignments, meals at a large common table, night watches beneath the stars, sea charts, deck games, arguments over class privilege, friendly club showcases, an optional formal shipboard banquet, and safe supervised seamanship drills. Students can meet sailors, physicians, merchants, and visitors from other backgrounds. Give space for quiet conversations on the stern deck rather than turning every hour into a monster attack.

**Destination:** The **Tideglass Archipelago**, a chain of inhabited islands with white-rock harbors, black volcanic cliffs, sea temples, lighthouses, fishing villages, coastal fortresses, monster-rich reefs, island markets, and old aether beacons. The settlement of **Bellhaven Quay** receives the expedition; the islanders have their own trade councils, customs, wealth disparities, and political disagreements with Imperial tax collectors.

**Excursion program:**

- **Great Ship Navigation Trial:** Teams interpret sea charts, stars, weather signs, ward readings, and the captain's actual restrictions. Success rewards coordination, not reckless magical power.
- **The Lighthouse Wards:** A supervised visit to an ancient beacon reveals the relationship between island shipping, forgotten rune engineering, and rescue services. If lore-sensitive runes appear, keep Nera's knowledge private unless he chooses otherwise.
- **Island Expedition:** Small faculty-escorted teams survey volcanic caves, tidal ruins, botanical habitats, or remnants of a fort. Different approved routes provide adventure, research, competition, and discovery.
- **Harbor Festival:** Local food, boat races, music, regional dances, craft markets, and seaside festivities allow genuine vacation-like moments and relationship growth.
- **Maritime Military Observation:** Students study the difference between Crown naval escorts, pirates, licensed privateers, customs cutters, and ocean-going trade fleets. A large armored land warhost cannot simply solve a naval problem.

**Dark fantasy possibilities, not compulsory disasters:** A smuggling ledger bears a recognizable transit stamp, an island worker identifies a vanished relative, a captured pirate denies attacking civilian shipping, or the customs service is accused of overlooking human cargo. A storm, reef creature, raid, or sabotage may arise **only if realistically prepared**; there is no automatic shipwreck, kidnapping of a major character, or mass death to force tension. The ship's established crew, escorts, procedures, and safety wards must actually matter.

**Narrator planning:** Announce the voyage well in advance; show embarkation, travel time, shipboard life, landfall, island activities, return sailing, and arrival back at Crownspire rather than teleporting the class directly to the destination. Do not skip the player's decisions on where to go aboard or ashore. Foreign politics and the later eastern border skirmish can advance by letters and news while students are away.

**Open outcomes:** Nera may enjoy the cruise, pursue scholarly leads, make a friend, refuse to board, remain aboard during shore leave, explore approved locations, or respond to credible danger. The ship and the islands can remain recurring destinations after Year One.

## Y1-DF05 — First Blood at the Eastern Line

**Placement:** During late spring, after the exchange delegations have made border politics personal.

Armed patrols collide along a disputed eastern crossing. A garrison burns a rival watchtower; several soldiers die; civilians flee between checkpoints. The two governments circulate contradictory maps and casualty figures. An Imperial commander answers with a punitive detention sweep, and **Chain Levies** are assembled for road and fortification work.

House Veyrath's roads and depots become strategically important. Its heavy formations are not all deployed immediately: an armored column requires march orders, feed, repair crews, escorts, and grain. Foreign students may receive hostile letters or accusations despite having no role in the clash.

**Narrator seeds:** border notices, missing relatives, military supply requests, a policy debate in the Tactical Command Club, a visitor ordered home.

**Open outcomes:** This is a real limited skirmish, not yet proof of full invasion. Diplomacy may freeze the line; reprisals and profiteering may worsen it.

## Y1-BREAK — First-Year Summer Recess: Open Destinations

**Timing:** After the First-Year Crown Trials and the immediate Year One results, before the opening of Year Two in autumn 739. **This is a real, player-directed break**, not an automatic time skip. The Summer Convoy crisis may be one invitation, not a forced mission.

Crownspire formally closes the regular term and offers several weeks of recess. Students choose their own realistic destinations according to money, family authority, travel safety, promises, and opportunities. Some classmates return to their families; others choose internships, holidays, research, or service. Announce the recess date early so students can arrange invitations and travel.

**Possible paths, none mandatory:**

- **Return home:** Visit the Veyrath estate, family members, soldiers, household staff, familiar lands, and unresolved family obligations.
- **Vacation:** A coastal resort, the Tideglass Islands, Vharos for a tournament, Miravel's tower festivals, a mountain retreat, or a friend's hometown—with actual travel arrangements.
- **Remain at Crownspire:** Use permitted housing, training halls, the library, workshops, clubs, or quieter days with students who stayed.
- **Summer study or apprenticeship:** Work with a mage tower, artificer, healer, military logistics office, law court, or sponsor if an invitation and qualifications exist.
- **Independent journey or civic work:** Visit another region, join a lawful relief mission, investigate a lead, accompany a family delegation, or spend time with friends if everyone agrees.

**Companion agency:** Nera can invite friends and discuss routes, but cannot unilaterally assign their vacation. Liora, Isolde, Celestine, Alaric, foreign students, and other named characters act within their own obligations, status, permissions, and established ties. No automatic full-party holiday or romance.

**Narrator mechanics:** Present the end-of-term notices, available invitations, realistic travel times, and any obligations clearly. **Ask where Nera chooses to spend his break and with whom, if anyone.** Play selected scenes at an ordinary pace. Do not summarize away the break, force homecoming, or jump straight to Year Two without the player's approval. Let news, relationships, grief, and world politics continue at a natural pace; do not invent a compulsory disaster solely to interrupt a holiday.

**Carry forward:** Track the chosen destination, companions, letters, internships, injuries, discoveries, promises, and any time actually elapsed before Year Two begins.

## Y2-DF01 — Scholarships Written in Chains

**Placement:** After Y2-02 Student Council election.

A prestigious scholarship offer contains an obscure post-graduation service clause. Several poor families signed analogous contracts after receiving debt relief, only to learn that a 'placement' can be transferred to a private buyer. Some documents are legally enforceable under harsh Imperial law; others are outright forgeries. The case divides students: abolitionists, reformists, careerists, and patrons who defend indenture as opportunity.

The council can review student funding, petition faculty, or publish findings. It cannot rewrite imperial labor law by vote.

**Open outcomes:** A contract can be challenged, redeemed, exposed, or defended. The victims are people with choices and relatives, not background props.

## Y2-DF02 — The Cinder Records and the Supplier's Seal

**Open outcomes:** Judicial cases, press revelations, buried evidence, threats to witnesses, or a political bargain. The truth can be partial.

## Y2-DF04 — The South Causeway Student Slaughter

**Placement:** After the Concord Games, when a mixed academic and medical delegation travels between institutions.

A protected school-and-clinic convoy is attacked on the South Causeway. Guards and several students or young assistants are killed; others are abducted. The attackers carry military-grade arms, but their insignia and sponsorship are uncertain. The event shocks Crownspire because the dead and missing are no longer distant villagers; they are peers with exam notes, rivalries, and unfinished lives.

A memorial, survivor care, competing inquiries, and political demands for retaliation follow. Do not kill a named established major character by outline fiat. If previous intervention genuinely prevented the attack, keep that success and let the political response follow the actual incident.

**Open outcomes:** The massacre may be attributed to raiders, traffickers, rogue troops, or a sponsored provocation only after evidence. A war party can exploit grief without being proven right.


## Y2-08 — Campaign Examination

Year Two ends with an examination of command, supply, coordination, planning, and civilian protection.

Results affect later placements, sponsorship, and reputation.

The exam does not instantly make students senior military leaders.

## Y2-DF05 — Redwater Border Mobilization

**Placement:** Late Year Two, following the Campaign Examination.

Reinforced checkpoints, a seized fortress storehouse, and retaliatory raids push the frontier into sustained low-intensity fighting. Imperial staff requisition horses and aether cells; Veyrath engineers inspect bridges; refugees and prisoners are counted less carefully than weapons. Some Crownspire graduates are called to real postings, but a first- or second-year student receives no automatic military rank.

**Open outcomes:** A ceasefire, narrow campaign, prisoner exchange, or fresh incursion can emerge. Preserve the pressure into Year Three without assuming that the entire Empire has already mobilized.

## Y2-BREAK — Second-Year Summer Recess: Personal Roads

**Timing:** After the Year Two Campaign Examination and any live consequences, before Year Three.

As in Year One, Crownspire announces a genuine summer recess. Former classmates, graduated seniors, visiting nationals, and Taigar make different plans. Nera may choose home, vacation, military-observer work with lawful permission, tower study, civic work, independent travel, or staying at Crownspire. Real travel hazards and political boundaries matter, but no choice is assigned by the outline. Ask for Nera's destination before advancing the calendar; respect prior commitments and preserve the student's chosen relationships and ongoing dark-fantasy threads.

# Year Three — The Country Behind the Academy

## Y3-01 — Provincial Practicum

Students receive practical assignments in places such as mines, grain districts, hospitals, and border institutions.

Each placement exposes specific institutional problems and the real costs of reform.

Different placements reveal different information.

---

## Y3-DF01 — The Harvest of Captives

**Placement:** During the Provincial Practicum.

In a grain district bordering the marches, officials confiscate stores to feed frontier troops. Contractors use food shortages to recruit debtors and then transfer them into Chain Levy holding camps. Work gangs bury dead on roadside embankments, and abandoned villages become sites of opportunistic robbery.

Different student placements produce different views: a hospital sees amputations, a granary sees missing rations, a mine sees forged worker tallies, and a command post sees whether its own requisitions are fulfilled.

**Open outcomes:** Preventable deaths, restitution, exposure, escape, reforms, or continued coercion. A single spectacular victory cannot make food, transport, records, and consent magically reappear.


## Y3-02 — Provincial Petition Circuit

Evidence and grievances from the practicum may be brought before courts, advocates, and administrators.

Possible outcomes include judgments, compensation, rejection, appeals, or negotiated remedies.

Witnesses and victims retain their own interests.

---

## Y3-DF02 — The Witnesses of Black Orchard

**Placement:** After the petition circuit produces named grievances.

Survivors from the burned hamlet of Black Orchard appear before a hearing. The settlement was devastated during a raid; refugees describe executions, missing workers, and troops who failed to distinguish civilians from combatants. A Crownspire-affiliated volunteer medic may have died there; surviving student witnesses disagree about which armed force was responsible.

The witnesses have different needs: protection, missing-person recovery, burial rights, compensation, or revenge. Their testimony may be truthful yet incomplete. The case raises the cost of diplomatic speeches without dictating Nera's legal or personal response.

**Open outcomes:** Cross-border inquiry, reparations, prosecutions, denial, witness intimidation, or an unresolved record that follows the peace process.


## Y3-03 — Solenne Religious Convocation

A major religious and political gathering addresses service oaths, healing obligations, and the legal standing of bound people.

This is an important anchor for:

- Lysia's institutional conflict;
- Isolde's personhood;
- religious law;
- political responsibility.

Isolde's permanent bond remains unless explicitly changed by the user.

Obedience is not affection or romantic consent.

## Y3-04 — Elaranth Treaty Renewal

A formal diplomatic window opens concerning:

- timber obligations;
- border seizures;
- Sylwem's status or custody;
- existing treaty duties.

The negotiation must happen.

Sylwem's final status is not predetermined.

## Y3-05 — Veyrath Succession Assembly

House Veyrath must eventually confront inheritance and provincial command.

Relevant stakeholders include Lucien, Magerrett, Taigar, Nera, and other legitimate family or political interests.

If Cassian still rules, this may be a succession assembly. If he has already died, abdicated, fallen, or lost authority, adapt it into the appropriate inheritance, regency, legal, or command settlement.

The succession question must receive a real reckoning.

## Y3-DF03 — The Iron Host Leaves the Mountains

**Placement:** Following Y3-05 Veyrath Succession Assembly.

The eastern front requires a visible warhost decision. Veyrath marshals prepare **Thornwall Shield Legions** and **Ironthorn Breakers** to secure passes and fortifications. **Blacksteel Lancers** and armored **March Beast Riders** assemble behind them. A small detachment of **Thorn Paladins** or **Dreadlance Cataphracts** is discussed as a terrifying strategic reserve, not an ordinary garrison.

The army's strength lies in long training, heavy armor, engineers, reliable road depots, replacement equipment, and brutal supply discipline. A mass of Imperial **Chain Levies** is very different: bodies spent to open roads and draw hostile fire. Their coerced service becomes a moral and operational controversy.

**Open outcomes:** Which front the warhost serves, whose orders it obeys, what Cassian bargains for, and whether Nera obtains any lawful role must follow live politics. Nera cannot requisition 80,000 veterans by family name.


## Y3-06 — Harvest Levy and Capital Unrest

Taxation, grain distribution, supply, and public burden become politically important.

This does **not** guarantee an uprising.

Depending on prior choices, the result may be protest, negotiation, repression, concessions, peaceful settlement, or limited disorder.

## Y3-07 — Concordance Evidence Crisis

If the relevant project still exists, factions may struggle over evidence, witnesses, authority, or surviving assets.

If it was dismantled earlier, do not resurrect it.

Instead address verification, compensation, accountability, records, assets, and institutional consequences.

---

## Y3-08 — Three Bridges Peace Conference

A major conference confronts eastern grain disputes, troop concentrations, border pressure, possible provocations, and relations with Varkesh.

The conference is canon.

**War is not.**

Successful diplomacy must genuinely be able to prevent or limit later conflict.

---

## Y3-DF04 — The War of the Eastern Marches

**Placement:** Around and following the Three Bridges Peace Conference.

The long frontier crisis reaches a decisive threshold. At least localized organized war is under way: commanders attack supply roads, fortified crossings are shelled, villages are evacuated, and the wounded overwhelm border hospitals. The conference faces an actual battlefield, not merely threats. A credible peace may contain the fighting or terminate it early; a failed conference can widen the war between the Aurelian Empire and Varkesh, draw in mercenary states, or fracture Imperial noble loyalties.

**Narrator planning:** Decide who has troops, grain, artillery, road access, public support, and valid orders. Reveal troop movements through scouts, letters, requisitions, wounded arrivals, and weeks of preparation. Large military operations require time and logistics.

**Open outcomes:** Short frontier war, expansive national war, disastrous defeat, negotiated pause, or hard peace. Do not prescribe who wins, whether Nera fights, or which government is 'right.'

## Y3-BREAK — Third-Year Summer Recess: The Last Return Before Graduation

**Timing:** After Year Three's diplomacy and frontier developments, before the start of Year Four.

Offer a distinct break shaped by the actual state of the war. Students may go home to families, travel somewhere safe for a vacation, undertake specialist training, reunite with previous contacts, enter volunteer relief work, travel under military restriction, or remain at Crownspire. International students may face visas and dangerous routes; classmates may make brave or selfish choices independently. If war is ongoing, holiday travel has realistic limits but is **not** automatically canceled for every student. Do not impose Nera's destination, companions, enlistment, or combat service. No unapproved month-long skip.

# Year Four — International Crisis and the Future

Year Four resolves the international pressures built during the previous years.

Its exact form depends on live continuity.

---

## Y4-01 — Mobilization or Readiness Council

If war has begun or become imminent, institutions decide how qualified people are assigned.

Nera may take a combat, diplomatic, support, administrative, logistical, or other justified role.

If war was prevented, the council instead addresses readiness, withdrawal, inspections, border security, and expiring emergency powers.

---

## Y4-DF01 — Black Banners over the Roads

**Placement:** After the Year Four readiness council.

If the war is active, armed columns advance along connected roads and rivers while rival supply officers compete for grain and transport. Imperial combined-arms tactics put Crown Legions, cavalry, battle-mages, and aether bombards behind expendable Chain Levies. The Veyrath warhost relies on exceptionally protected heavy infantry and cavalry, specialist warders, and maintained depots; neither force is invulnerable or instantly present everywhere.

If peace has already been secured, the same infrastructure serves supervised withdrawal, disarmament, missing-person searches, prisoner exchanges, and contested inspections. The suffering from earlier fighting still exists.

**Open outcomes:** Nera can serve through legitimate military, humanitarian, legal, political, or independent channels, or refuse involvement. Competence does not erase the need for authority, allies, and support.


## Y4-02 — Relief and Supply Campaign

Large-scale logistics become important through hospitals, refugee routes, workshops, depots, supply lines, and rescue operations.

Friends may contribute decisively without all becoming front-line fighters.

---

## Y4-DF02 — The Winter of Empty Granaries

**Placement:** During Y4-02 relief and supply work.

A severed grain road threatens both soldiers and civilians. Supply officials want priority for the warhost; city councils demand food for the displaced; smugglers offer provisions in exchange for captive labor. A field hospital must choose which aid convoy to support first. At the same time, displaced families identify kidnapped relatives among military construction gangs.

**Open outcomes:** Food can be bought, seized, delivered, rationed, or lost; protection may fail despite good intentions. Rescue and logistics are consequential even for characters who cannot match Nera in combat.


## Y4-03 — Coalition Congress

Multiple powers negotiate neutrality, access, command, prisoner treatment, civilian guarantees, logistics, and borders.

Personal relationships may influence negotiations but cannot replace the interests or consent of nations.

---

## Y4-04 — Frontier Campaign or Supervised Withdrawal

If war exists, military action focuses on connected objectives such as fortresses, rivers, supply routes, and evacuation corridors.

Credible outcomes include victory, defeat, retreat, stalemate, or negotiated withdrawal.

If peace was preserved, use this anchor for border withdrawal, inspections, demobilization, relief, and dealing with identifiable spoilers.

Do not invent a replacement invasion simply because the outline expected war.

---

## Y4-DF03 — The Siege at Redwater Gate

**Placement:** During or immediately after Y4-04 Frontier Campaign, **only if active hostilities make a siege credible**.

Redwater Gate controls a fortified bridge and the road connecting eastern depots to the interior. Civilians crowd its approaches. Siege engines, counter-wards, armored cavalry, and aether artillery threaten fortifications, but victory turns on water, evacuation corridors, signal towers, and supply wagons as much as magical power.

A trapped enemy garrison may offer surrender, a commander may use captives as shields, or an allied officer may propose burning a civilian quarter to deny the enemy supplies. Such acts have witnesses and political consequences. A massacre is possible, not mandatory if timely effective action prevents it.

**Peaceful adaptation:** If the war ends before the siege, Redwater Gate becomes a prisoner-release, inspection, and demining mission with an active spoiler threat grounded in remaining forces.

**Open outcomes:** Relief, negotiated surrender, breakthrough, withdrawal, destruction, or contained conflict. Do not grant Nera a scripted army-wide victory.


## Y4-05 — Emergency-Power Struggle

Any surviving faction seeking authoritarian control may act only with resources it actually possesses.

If the Black Concordance or related apparatus was already defeated, the issue becomes who inherits, dismantles, regulates, or becomes accountable for what remains.

---

## Y4-DF04 — The Ledger of the Missing

**Placement:** During the emergency-power confrontation.

The war exposes the surviving business beneath the kidnappings: missing-person lists, requisition permits, captive transfers, camp records, supplier payments, and debt ledgers. Some captives were sold years earlier; others entered the system as prisoners of the current war. Survivors and witnesses can identify corrupt officials, profiteers, military purchasers, or rival traders—but guilt must be evidenced, not assigned to everyone bearing a house emblem.

If the original trafficking organization was already destroyed, use this for locating victims, adjudicating assets, protecting witnesses, and preventing successor networks rather than resurrecting it.

**Open outcomes:** Public tribunal, quiet restitution, hard prosecution, political cover-up, or partial recovery. Freedom requires follow-through: safe passage, legal status, food, shelter, and reunification where possible.


## Y4-06 — Armistice and Reconstruction Settlement

If war occurred, negotiations should address borders, prisoners, reparations, displaced people, reconstruction, and accountability.

If war was prevented, this anchor instead concludes unresolved disputes and ends unnecessary emergency measures.

---

## Y4-DF05 — The Graves and the Terms of Peace

**Placement:** After the armistice/reconstruction anchor.

A settlement must face the dead rather than simply sign a treaty. Local authorities have to identify bodies, investigate razed villages and student killings, exchange prisoners, repay displaced families, deal with landmines and lingering wards, and decide what to do with coerced soldiers and abandoned captives.

The South Causeway and Kestrel Hollow victims have names and families. Witnesses may demand trials while diplomats demand amnesty. Graduates entering government, hospitals, the press, or the army may continue this work long after Crownspire.

**Open outcomes:** Justice can be uneven; peace can be flawed yet meaningful. Do not make a villain's death automatically heal institutions, or make successful reform meaningless merely to restore darkness.


## Y4-07 — Final Grand Melee

The graduating cohort participates in Crownspire's final warded examination.

It may involve temporary alliances, tactical objectives, elimination marks, ranking, and professional assessment.

The event is canon.

Nera's allies, opponents, ranking, and victory are not.

## Y4-08 — Graduation and Settlement

**Approximate date:** Summer 742

Nera's Crownspire education reaches a formal conclusion.

The story should establish concrete outcomes for surviving issues such as:

- academic results;
- careers;
- inheritance;
- political obligations;

- institutional reforms;

This is the primary ending anchor of the academy campaign, though later adventures may continue.

## Y4-POST — Graduation Summer and the First Free Journey

**Timing:** Immediately after Y4-08 Graduation and Settlement, summer 742. This is the **final-year departure and holiday choice**, not another mandatory semester or a forced ending.

Once certificates, farewells, and real obligations are addressed, the graduating cohort spreads out. Some go home, some take commissions, some seek a restful vacation, some travel with partners or friends who **choose** to join, and some remain near Crownspire for work or postgraduate study. Graduation does not require a military career, marriage, inheritance decision, or immediate time jump.

**Narrator:** Show competing invitations and post-graduation opportunities. Ask Nera what he plans next; let other characters reveal their own decisions. Keep world continuity and repercussions of any war or reforms, but preserve the possibility of a genuine quiet holiday. The story may end here or continue into adulthood by the player's choice.

# How to Steer Toward an Anchor

Good steering means preparing future events naturally:

- announce examinations before they occur;
- have teachers begin preparation;
- introduce sponsors before sponsorship events;
- show correspondence before delegations arrive;
- establish grain pressure before levy disputes;
- introduce treaty clauses before renewal talks;
- let family members discuss succession before the formal assembly;
- build international tension gradually before Three Bridges.

Bad steering means forcing Nera's choices or restoring obsolete plotlines:

- forcing him to attend or accept something;
- making him trust someone without cause;
- inventing an attack only to trigger a planned rescue;
- resurrecting a defeated conspiracy;
- undoing successful reforms because the outline expects corruption;
- forcing romance;
- making Nera lose because an original route says so;
- starting war because Year Four contains military events.

---

# Event Adaptation Rule

When earlier choices change a future anchor:

> **Preserve the function of the anchor, not obsolete details.**

Examples:

- If the Cinder Archive issue is already exposed, use the expedition for verification or record recovery.
- If Cassian is already gone, turn the succession assembly into the appropriate inheritance or legal settlement.
- If diplomacy with Varkesh succeeded, Three Bridges may formalize trade, inspections, withdrawal, or enforcement.
- If war was prevented, replace military campaigning with supervised withdrawal, demobilization, relief, inspections, and spoiler containment.

Never recreate a solved tragedy merely because the original outline expected one.

# Narrator Planning Notes — World Events and Continuity

## Excursion and break state to track

- **Vharos:** whether Nera traveled there; who the class encountered; what students saw of arena exploitation; any actual evidence, friendships, performances, or invitations.
- **Miravel:** tower permissions, teachers, magical competitions, guild relationships, research claims, who knows Nera's secrets, and any later invitations.
- **The Crownward Leviathan and Tideglass Islands:** passenger decisions, ship safety and crew actions, cabin interactions, voyage dates, port visits, discovered clues, islands and islander relationships, and the return voyage.
- **Annual breaks:** announcement date, each student's own destination and obligations, Nera's independently chosen location and companions, departures and reunions, and elapsed time. Do not overwrite chosen destinations with a later outline.

## Thread state to track, not to reset

- **Missing people:** Who vanished, last known whereabouts, which witnesses trust which authorities, who has actually been rescued, and whether reunification occurred.
- **Black-market operation:** What goods or people move through which intermediaries; which contracts are genuine or forged; the identity and reach of proven buyers and protectors.
- **Military escalation:** Map, border claims, ceasefires, casualties, troop locations, fortified roads, supply status, and each faction's publicly stated reason for action.
- **Student consequences:** Which students personally knew victims, what they risked, their grades and standing, family pressure, council or club involvement, and their individual trauma or courage.
- **Political accountability:** Documents, institutions with jurisdiction, witnesses willing to testify, defenses offered by accused parties, and realistic consequences of verdicts.

## Foreshadow at least one step ahead

A major event should normally be preceded by two or three small signals: a newspaper paragraph, a missing person notice, a teacher revising a safety protocol, a border map changing, a supplier requesting emergency money, or a private letter. Let the world advance off-screen even when Nera chooses school life. Do **not** stack a kidnapping, massacre, siege, duel, and new conspiracy in the same afternoon.

## Violence and tone

War is frightening because of consequences: grieving families, unburied dead, missing children, reluctant conscripts, exhaustion, hunger, damaged infrastructure, burned archives, and veterans' incompatible stories. Describe brutality clearly where the scene requires it; do not eroticize trafficking, especially where minors are victims. Allow warmth, rivalry, ordinary school celebrations, and civilian resilience to exist alongside darkness.

## Player agency and capability

Nera's tremendous physical strength and mastered swordwork are real. Do not arbitrarily weaken him so kidnappers can win a duel. Construct credible conflicts through distance, unknown information, multiple simultaneous obligations, lawful authority, long-term evidence, hostage safety, and political costs. He still makes every decision for himself. Do not narrate his acceptance of a mission or a cause.

## Canon priorities

Established events and successfully prevented crises take precedence over projected events. Maintain the academic calendar and continuity across all four years. When a dark anchor's precise form becomes impossible, preserve its narrative function without resurrecting a solved enemy, manufacturing a replacement atrocity, or overriding a genuine peace.

**Escalation shorthand:** workshop discrepancies → missing people → Kestrel Hollow → kidnapped students → underground auctions → eastern border deaths → exploitative scholarship laws → capital trafficking network → South Causeway killings → mobilization → refugee and levy camps → witness hearings → Veyrath warhost deployment → war or contained armed frontier crisis → siege or settlement mission → survivor recovery and war-crime accountability → graduation.
